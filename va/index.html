Order pipeline that answered "where is my order"

Import orders came in through a WhatsApp group and were tracked on a spreadsheet. Because shipping runs through a private consolidator there was no courier tracking number, so the team hand-answered the same question all day.

I mapped how fulfilment actually works, then set up a seven-stage pipeline: Order Confirmed, Purchased, In Transit, Arrived in Ghana, Clearing Customs, Ready for Delivery, Delivered. Customers now look their own order up instead of asking, and there is one place to see where every order stands.

A customer service system, written down

Every enquiry landed in one inbox, from "how do I start a business" to "where is my order", and each got a hand-typed reply whose quality depended on who was free.

I classified what people actually ask into nine intents, then wrote 63 workflows in Goal, Steps, Outcome format, with escalation points and rules the replies cannot break: no promising profits, no inventing shipping dates. It is the difference between an inbox that depends on one person and one anybody can be trained into in a day.

Onboarding

The first two weeks, so you know what you are signing up for.

Week one, days 1 to 2

A single call, then I watch how things currently run. I do not change anything yet. Most of what goes wrong in handovers is someone rebuilding a process before understanding why it exists.

Week one, days 3 to 5

I write down what you actually do, in your words, and agree the rules: what I answer without asking, what I escalate, what I never touch. That document is the whole relationship.

Week two

I take the inbox and the calendar with you watching. You correct me in real time. Corrections go straight into the rules, so the same thing is never wrong twice.

From week three

You stop watching. Weekly check-ins, a monthly report, and your involvement drops to approving things rather than doing them.

Questions people actually ask

What about the time difference?

I am on GMT in Accra, which covers a full European working day and US mornings. If you are in London we overlap entirely. If you are in New York you wake up to work that is already done.

How do you handle access to my accounts?

Through a password manager you own, with access you can revoke in one click. Never shared passwords over chat. I ask for the narrowest permission that does the job, and I write down what I was given so offboarding takes five minutes.

What if it is not working?

The trial week exists for that. After that, thirty days' notice either way, and everything I have written stays yours: the SOPs, the templates, the rules. You are not locked in by me being the only one who knows how things run.

Why not hire someone cheaper?

You can, and for pure data entry you probably should. What you are paying for here is someone who has run these functions for real businesses and builds the system rather than only working inside it. If your problem is volume, hire cheap. If it is that things keep falling through, that is a different job.
