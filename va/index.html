How a month works

Every Monday

Inbox cleared to zero, week's priorities agreed in a short written check-in. You approve the plan, then stop thinking about it.

Daily

Enquiries answered inside the response rules we set. Orders moved along the pipeline. Anything that needs you is flagged once, batched, not drip-fed.

The content calendar is drafted a fortnight ahead, so nothing is written in a panic. You review a batch, I schedule it and handle the replies.

A customer service system, written down

Every enquiry landed in one inbox, from "how do I start a business" to "where is my order", and each got a hand-typed reply whose quality depended on who was free.

Onboarding

The first two weeks, so you know what you are signing up for.

Questions people actually ask

What about the time difference?

I am on GMT in Accra and my working day runs to 8pm local. That covers a full London day, a full European one, and New York from 9am to 4pm. If you are in London we overlap entirely. If you are in New York, I am online before you start and still there mid-afternoon. Booking times on my calendar are shown in your timezone, not mine.

How do you handle access to my accounts?

Through a password manager you own, with access you can revoke in one click. Never shared passwords over chat. I ask for the narrowest permission that does the job, and I write down what I was given so offboarding takes five minutes. I handle confidential business and personal information with discretion, and I assume everything I see is confidential unless you tell me otherwise.

What if it is not working?

The trial week exists for that. After that, thirty days' notice either way, and everything I have written stays yours: the SOPs, the templates, the rules. You are not locked in by me being the only one who knows how things run.

What happens when a brief is vague?

If you send me "look into this and get it moving", I write back one line with what I think you meant, then get on with it. Most of the cost of a vague brief is the day lost waiting for clarification, so I make my assumption visible rather than stalling you with a question. You get options, a recommendation and what I am doing next, not a list to sort through yourself.

Why not hire someone cheaper?

You can, and for pure data entry you probably should. What you are paying for here is someone who has run these functions for real businesses and builds the system rather than only working inside it. If your problem is volume, hire cheap. If it is that things keep falling through, that is a different job.
